# Biology 201: Supplementary Notes

## Enzyme kinetics

Michaelis-Menten kinetics describes how reaction rate depends on substrate concentration. Km is the concentration at half of Vmax.

## Membrane transport

Facilitated diffusion moves glucose down its gradient through GLUT transporters, while the sodium potassium pump spends ATP to move ions against theirs.

## Osmosis in plant cells

A plant cell in a hypotonic solution becomes turgid rather than bursting, because the cell wall resists the pressure.

## Chlorophyll pigments

Chlorophyll a and b absorb strongly in the blue and red, and carotenoids extend the range while protecting against excess light.

## Stomatal regulation

Guard cells swell and open the pore when potassium ions flow in, and close it when abscisic acid signals water stress.

## Water transport in xylem

The cohesion tension theory explains how transpiration at the leaf pulls a continuous column of water up the xylem.

## Phloem loading

Sucrose is loaded into sieve tubes at the source and unloaded at the sink, and the resulting pressure gradient drives mass flow.

## Nitrogen fixation

Rhizobium bacteria in root nodules reduce atmospheric nitrogen to ammonia using nitrogenase, an enzyme poisoned by oxygen.

## Cell cycle checkpoints

The G1 checkpoint asks whether the cell is large enough and the DNA undamaged before committing to replication.

## Mitosis stages

Prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase, with the spindle attaching at kinetochores and the chromatids separating.

## Meiosis and variation

Crossing over in prophase I and independent assortment in metaphase I are what make gametes different from one another.

## DNA replication

Replication is semiconservative, with the leading strand synthesised continuously and the lagging strand in Okazaki fragments.

## Transcription

RNA polymerase binds the promoter and synthesises a messenger RNA from the template strand, five prime to three prime.

## Translation

The ribosome reads codons and tRNA brings the matching amino acid, with the peptide bond formed in the peptidyl transferase centre.

## Operons

The lac operon is switched off by a repressor until lactose is present, an early and still useful example of gene regulation.

## Mutation types

A point mutation may be silent, missense or nonsense, and a frameshift from an insertion changes everything downstream.

## Natural selection

Differential reproductive success acting on heritable variation, illustrated by the peppered moth in industrial England.

## Speciation

Allopatric speciation follows geographic isolation, while sympatric speciation happens without it, often through polyploidy in plants.

## Ecological succession

Pioneer species colonise bare rock, soil accumulates, and the community moves toward a climax that a disturbance can reset.

## Trophic levels

Only about ten per cent of the energy at one level reaches the next, which is why food chains are short.

## The carbon cycle

Carbon moves between the atmosphere, living tissue, soil and the ocean, with combustion of fossil carbon as the recent addition.

## The nitrogen cycle

Fixation, nitrification, assimilation and denitrification, with fertiliser runoff as the human thumb on the scale.

## Population growth

Exponential growth gives way to logistic growth as the population approaches the carrying capacity of its environment.

## Predator prey cycles

Lynx and hare populations oscillate with a lag, the classic illustration of a coupled dynamic system in ecology.

## Homeostasis

Negative feedback keeps body temperature, blood glucose and osmolarity within range; positive feedback is rarer and usually terminal or reproductive.

## The nephron

Filtration at the glomerulus, reabsorption in the proximal tubule, and concentration through the counter current in the loop of Henle.

## Gas exchange in the lung

Alveoli present an enormous surface, and the partial pressure gradient does the rest without any pumping of gases.

## Haemoglobin

The oxygen dissociation curve is sigmoid because of cooperative binding, and it shifts right with carbon dioxide and heat.

## The heart cycle

Systole and diastole, with the sinoatrial node setting the pace and the valves preventing backflow.

## Nerve impulses

The action potential is an all or nothing event, with sodium in then potassium out, propagated by saltatory conduction between nodes of Ranvier.

## Synapses

Neurotransmitter released into the cleft binds receptors on the postsynaptic membrane and is then removed, which is where most drugs act.

## The immune response

Innate defences act first and without memory; the adaptive response is slower, specific, and is what vaccination trains.

## Antibiotic resistance

Selection acts on the bacteria that survive a partial course, which is why finishing the prescription matters.

## Viruses

A virus is not a cell, and the lytic and lysogenic cycles are the two ways it uses one.

## Fungi and decomposition

Saprotrophic fungi secrete enzymes and absorb the products, returning nitrogen and carbon to the soil.

## Plant hormones

Auxin, gibberellin, cytokinin, abscisic acid and ethylene, with phototropism the classic demonstration of the first.

## Seed germination

Imbibition, mobilisation of the endosperm reserves, and emergence, with temperature and light as the usual triggers.

## Photoperiodism

Short day plants flower when the night exceeds a critical length, which is what the name gets backwards.

## Laboratory safety

Goggles for anything heated or corrosive, no mouth pipetting, and a documented procedure for spills and for broken glass.

## Microscopy

Magnification is not resolution, and the limit of a light microscope is set by the wavelength of light, not by the lenses.
