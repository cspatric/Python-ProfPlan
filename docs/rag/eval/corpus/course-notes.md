# Biology 201: Cell Energetics

## Photosynthesis: the light-dependent reactions

In the thylakoid membrane, photosystem II absorbs a photon at 680 nm and splits
water, releasing oxygen as a by-product. The electron travels down the transport
chain to photosystem I, and the proton gradient built across the membrane drives
ATP synthase.

## The Calvin cycle

The Calvin cycle fixes carbon dioxide using RuBisCO, the most abundant enzyme on
Earth. Three turns of the cycle produce one molecule of glyceraldehyde
3-phosphate. RuBisCO also binds oxygen, which is where photorespiration comes
from.

## C4 and CAM adaptations

Maize and sugarcane concentrate carbon dioxide in bundle sheath cells before
fixation, which suppresses photorespiration in hot climates. CAM plants such as
pineapple and agave open their stomata only at night.

## Van Helmont and the origin of plant mass

Jan Baptista van Helmont grew a willow for five years. The tree gained 74 kg
while the soil lost only 57 grams. He concluded, wrongly, that the mass came
from water; it comes from carbon dioxide in the air.

## Priestley, Ingenhousz and the discovery of oxygen

Joseph Priestley showed in 1771 that a mint sprig could restore air spoiled by a
candle. Jan Ingenhousz demonstrated in 1779 that the effect required sunlight
and that plants in the dark do the opposite.

## Glycolysis

Glycolysis splits one molecule of glucose into two of pyruvate in the cytosol,
with a net yield of two ATP and two NADH. It needs no oxygen, which is why it is
shared by nearly every organism alive.

## The citric acid cycle

Also called the Krebs cycle, it runs in the mitochondrial matrix. Each turn
releases two molecules of carbon dioxide and yields three NADH, one FADH2 and
one GTP. Hans Krebs described it in 1937.

## Oxidative phosphorylation

Complexes I through IV pump protons into the intermembrane space, and ATP
synthase uses the gradient to make roughly 30 ATP per glucose. Cyanide blocks
complex IV, which is why it kills so quickly.

## Fermentation

Without oxygen, yeast converts pyruvate into ethanol and carbon dioxide, and
muscle converts it into lactate. Neither yields more ATP than glycolysis
already produced; both exist to regenerate NAD+.

## Chloroplast and mitochondrion as former bacteria

The endosymbiotic theory, argued by Lynn Margulis in 1967, holds that both
organelles descend from free-living prokaryotes. Their own circular DNA and
double membranes are the evidence usually cited.

## Common student misconceptions

Students routinely believe plants take their mass from the soil, that
respiration happens only at night, and that photosynthesis is the opposite of
respiration rather than a different pathway with a different purpose.
